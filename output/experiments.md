### Differential bacteria and the LEfSe biomarkers between groups
The Kruskal-Wallis test was used to identify the differential bacteria among the five groups. The relative abundance of _Lactobacillus_ and _Streptococcus_ showed significant differences, with _Lactobacillus_ exhibiting the highest proportion in the HE group, followed by the ME, HRT, LE, and NC groups, whereas _Streptococcus_ showed the opposite trend (Fig. 2A).

### Vaginal community state types (CSTs) distribution among groups
Based on the composition and abundance of vaginal microbiota, the vaginal microbiome was classified into five community state types (CSTs), including four _Lactobacillus_ -dominant CSTs (CST I: _L. crispatus_ -dominated; CST II: _L. gasseri_ -dominated; CST III: _L. iners_ -dominated; CST V: _L. jensenii_ -dominated) and one non-_Lactobacillus_ -dominant CST (CST IV: diverse anaerobes-dominated) [18]. The distribution of CSTs among the five groups is shown in Fig. 2E. The _Lactobacillus_ -dominant CSTs were predominant across all groups, with the highest proportion observed in the HE group, followed by the ME, HRT, LE, and NC groups.
### Correlation between estradiol levels and vaginal microbiota
The correlation between the estradiol levels and the vaginal microbiota was analyzed using Spearman's correlation analysis. The relative abundance of _Streptococcus anginosus_ , _Akkermansia_ , and _Gardnerella_ was positively correlated with the peak estradiol levels (_p_ < 0.05). In contrast, the relative abundance of _Escherichia-Shigella_ and _Klebsiella_ was negatively correlated with the peak estradiol levels (_p_ < 0.05) (Fig. 3A).

### Pregnancy outcomes
Clinical pregnancy was confirmed in 42 out of 92 patients (45.7%). The clinical pregnancy rates were 53.3% (8/15), 40.0% (4/10), 56.3% (9/16), 46.2% (12/26), and 36.0% (9/25) in the NC, HRT, LE, ME, and HE groups, respectively, with no significant differences among the groups (_p_ = 0.659).
The alpha diversity of the vaginal microbiota was not significantly different between pregnant and non-pregnant patients (Fig. 3B). The beta diversity also showed no significant difference between the two groups (Adonis _p_ = 0.210) (Fig. 3C). The composition of vaginal microbiota at the genus level between the two groups is shown in Fig. 3D. LEfSe analysis was conducted to identify the biomarkers between the two groups. The family _Atopobiaceae_ and the genus _Atopobium_ were enriched in the non-pregnant group (_LDA ≥ 2.0_ , _p_ < 0.05) (Fig. 3E).
### Predictive value of vaginal microbiota in pregnancy outcomes
ROC analysis was conducted to evaluate the predictive value of vaginal microbiota in pregnancy outcomes. The abundance of _Streptococcus_ , _Atopobium_ , and _Bifidobacterium_ on the day of embryo transfer showed a certain predictive value for adverse pregnancy outcomes. The AUC values of _Streptococcus_ , _Atopobium_ , and _Bifidobacterium_ were 0.704, 0.706, and 0.708, respectively. The combined AUC value was 0.722 (Fig. 3F).
## Discussion
In the present study, we found that the vaginal microbiome exhibited variations with changes in peak estradiol levels, both during ovarian stimulation and exogenous estrogen supplementation. The elevated estradiol levels significantly reduced alpha diversity, altered beta diversity within the vaginal microbiome, and shifted the vaginal CSTs in Chinese infertile women toward _Lactobacillus_ -dominant profiles, resembling those observed in most Asian women previously [19]. However, the reproductive outcomes were not improved by these variations. The _Streptococcus anginosus_ and _Akkermansia_ abundance correlated with estradiol levels positively, whereas _Escherichia-Shigella_ showed a negative correlation. The abundance of _Streptococcus_ , _Atopobium_ , and _Bifidobacterium_ on the day of embryo transfer may serve as predictors for adverse pregnancy outcomes. Among them, _Streptococcus_ correlates positively with peak estradiol levels and may act as a microbial mediator impairing reproductive success under hyperestrogenic conditions.
The vaginal microbiome plays a crucial role in maintaining vaginal health and reproductive success. The colonization of the female reproductive tract by _Lactobacillus_ is associated with estrogen levels [1]. Estrogen can stimulate the proliferation and maturation of vaginal epithelial cells, promote glycogen deposition, and create an acidic environment that favors the growth of _Lactobacillus_ [3]. The IVF-ET process can induce a maternal supraphysiological estradiol environment during embryo implantation and early development [4]. In the present study, we found that the vaginal microbiome exhibited variations with changes in peak estradiol levels, both during ovarian stimulation and exogenous estrogen supplementation. The elevated estradiol levels significantly reduced alpha diversity, altered beta diversity within the vaginal microbiome, and shifted the vaginal CSTs in Chinese infertile women toward _Lactobacillus_ -dominant profiles. These findings are consistent with previous studies [20, 21], which have shown that estrogen can modulate the vaginal microbiome and promote the colonization of _Lactobacillus_.
The _Lactobacillus_ -dominant microbiome profile has been found to be positively correlated with reproductive success [2]. However, in the present study, we found that the reproductive outcomes were not improved by the _Lactobacillus_ -dominant profiles induced by supraphysiological estradiol levels. This finding suggests that the _Lactobacillus_ dominance under supraphysiological estradiol conditions does not help improve assisted reproductive outcomes. The possible reasons may be as follows: First, the supraphysiological estradiol levels may have adverse effects on embryo implantation and early development [4, 10,11,12,13]. Second, the _Lactobacillus_ -dominant profiles induced by supraphysiological estradiol levels may not be the same as those observed in healthy reproductive-age women. Third, the _Lactobacillus_ -dominant profiles may not be the only factor affecting reproductive success. Other factors, such as the endometrial receptivity, embryo quality, and immune status, may also play important roles.
In the present study, we found that the abundance of _Streptococcus_ , _Atopobium_ , and _Bifidobacterium_ on the day of embryo transfer may serve as predictors for adverse pregnancy outcomes. Among them, _Streptococcus_ correlates positively with peak estradiol levels and may act as a microbial mediator impairing reproductive success under hyperestrogenic conditions. _Streptococcus_ is a common commensal bacterium in the vagina, but some species, such as _Streptococcus anginosus_ , have been associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes [22, 23]. The possible mechanisms may include the production of inflammatory cytokines, the disruption of the vaginal epithelial barrier, and the induction of immune responses [22, 23]. _Atopobium_ is another common commensal bacterium in the vagina, but it has been associated with bacterial vaginosis and adverse pregnancy outcomes [24, 25]. _Bifidobacterium_ is a probiotic bacterium that has been shown to have beneficial effects on vaginal health [26]. However, in the present study, we found that the abundance of _Bifidobacterium_ on the day of embryo transfer was associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes. The possible reasons may be that the _Bifidobacterium_ species detected in the present study may not be the same as those with beneficial effects, or that the _Bifidobacterium_ abundance may be a marker of dysbiosis rather than a causative factor.
The present study has several limitations. First, the sample size was relatively small, and the results need to be validated in larger cohorts. Second, the study was conducted in a single center, and the results may not be generalizable to other populations. Third, the study only examined the vaginal microbiome at a single timepoint, and the dynamic changes in the vaginal microbiome during the IVF-ET process were not investigated. Fourth, the study only examined the bacterial composition of the vaginal microbiome, and the functional characteristics of the vaginal microbiome were not investigated. Fifth, the study did not investigate the mechanisms by which supraphysiological estradiol levels modulate the vaginal microbiome and affect reproductive outcomes. Further studies are needed to address these limitations and elucidate the potential mechanisms.
## Conclusions
Supraphysiological estradiol levels induced by IVF-ET significantly alter vaginal microbiota and shift the CSTs in Chinese infertile women toward patterns of most Asian women. The _Lactobacillus_ dominance under supraphysiological estradiol conditions does not help improve assisted reproductive outcomes. The abundance of _Streptococcus_ , _Atopobium_ , and _Bifidobacterium_ on the day of embryo transfer may serve as predictors for adverse pregnancy outcomes. Among them, _Streptococcus_ correlates positively with peak estradiol levels and may act as a microbial mediator impairing reproductive success under hyperestrogenic conditions. However, further larger-scale researches are needed to identify and elucidate the potential mechanisms.
